Where the lines come from

Each line is one item on one invoice. Data runs from 1 Dec 2010 to about 9 Dec 2011.

One in four lines has no customer ID

Of every 100 lines, 25 have no customer ID

Share of raw lines affected

These lines still count for sales, products, countries and months. They are left out only when looking at individual customers.

I removed only what was clearly repeated

5,268Removed. Exact duplicate lines. 541,909 lines became 536,641.
1,342Fixed. Missing product names, filled in from the same product elsewhere. 112 remain blank.
9,251Kept and marked. Cancelled lines, so returns count against revenue. Odd prices were also kept and marked.

10.64M of sales became 9.73M of net revenue

Cancellations take back 8.4% of sales. The small last step is two accounting entries labelled "Adjust bad debt".

Best sellers by volume are not always the biggest earners

Most units sold

Most revenue

Only 5 products appear in both top 10 lists (solid bars). These counts are sales before returns.

Nearly a third of the top earners are not products

Postage and manual entries rank among the top 10 "products". The top real product is the Regency Cakestand 3 Tier at 174,157.

Cancellations are few lines, but many invoices

Of every 100 invoices, about 15 are cancellations

Share affected

3,836 of 25,900 invoices are cancellations, but only 1.7% of lines.

Two top sellers were almost entirely sent back

Paper craft, little birdie

Medium ceramic top storage jar

Units sold and units returned are nearly equal. This looks like large orders that were reversed, so these two products overstate real demand.

A few invoices are far larger than the rest

Solid bars are invoices with a single product. 99% of invoices are below 4,107.

The United Kingdom is 85% of sales

The next nine countries

Revenue climbed from September to November

November 2011 was the peak at 1.46M. September to November made up 36% of the year. December 2011 looks low only because the data ends around 9 December. One year is not enough to call it seasonal.

Top 10 customers by total spend

How often vs how much per order

The top 10 customers bring in 14% of net revenue. Some order often in small baskets, others rarely in large ones.

What I would do next

  1. Report products without postage and manual entriesThey distort the rankings.
  2. Count returns against productsRankings should show sales after returns.
  3. Check the autumn peak against a second yearOne year cannot show a pattern.
  4. Treat frequent and big-basket customers differentlyThey buy in different ways.
  5. Find out why 25% of lines have no customer IDIt limits what we can learn about customers.

Proposals from this analysis. None have been put into practice.

Cleaning the data changed the story

At face value, the top product is postage and a top seller is a reversed order. Checking first gave a more reliable picture.

Notes on the data: country invoice counts in the notebook are unreliable, so only country revenue is shown. December 2011 is a partial month. Product rankings are before returns. Interpretations here are mine and not proven by the data.
